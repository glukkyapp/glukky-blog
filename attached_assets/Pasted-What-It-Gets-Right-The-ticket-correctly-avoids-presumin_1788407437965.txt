What It Gets Right
The ticket correctly avoids presuming a fix before diagnosis. Step 1 frames the investigation as "confirm which surface is missing its label" across three candidate layers — the static HTML/native-splash interval, the cold-launch cube, and the delayed global overlay — rather than jumping straight to a patch. That's the right instinct, since "works in Replit preview, fails on the real iPhone" almost always means the bug is in something the preview browser doesn't reproduce.

The "Done looks like" constraint about not depending on "a remote font finishing or an animated state" is a reasonable defensive requirement even before the trace confirms it — text that fades in via animation or waits on a webfont is a classic cause of invisible-but-present text on real devices (iOS throttles animations differently, and slow network delays webfont loading in a way local preview never experiences).

What It Likely Misses
Viewport/safe-area clipping is the more common cause for this exact symptom, and it's not mentioned. "Visible in Replit preview, invisible on a real iPhone" is the textbook signature of a layout issue tied to the device's actual screen geometry — notch/status-bar safe-area insets, iOS Safari's collapsing toolbar changing 100vh behavior, or standalone/home-screen-installed mode rendering fixed-position elements differently than a browser tab. None of the three traced surfaces in Step 1 (native-splash interval, cube, global overlay) address whether the label is being rendered but positioned off-screen or under the status bar on that specific device. This is worth adding as a fourth thing to check during the trace, alongside font/animation timing.

The Natively/BuildNatively exclusion may rule out the actual mechanism before diagnosis confirms it's unrelated. The ticket explicitly lists "Natively/BuildNatively bridge behavior" as out of scope, but the deployed iPhone app is almost certainly running inside that bridge/WebView wrapper (that's presumably why "the Replit preview" — a plain browser — behaves differently from "the deployed app" on a physical device). If the bridge's WebView handles safe-area insets, splash-screen dismissal timing, or content clipping differently than a bare browser, that's squarely inside the failure path being investigated, not a separate concern. Excluding it from scope before Step 1's trace is complete risks ruling out the real cause by definition rather than by evidence.

Suggested Fix to the Ticket
Add a fourth trace target to Step 1:

"Check whether the label is present in the DOM but visually clipped or mispositioned due to iOS safe-area insets, standalone/WebView viewport height behavior, or bridge-layer content inset, in addition to the animation/font-dependency hypothesis."

And soften the bridge exclusion to something like:

"Changes to Natively/BuildNatively bridge configuration are out of scope, unless the trace in Step 1 shows the bridge's WebView layer is the direct cause of the missing label, in which case the minimal necessary adjustment is in scope."
