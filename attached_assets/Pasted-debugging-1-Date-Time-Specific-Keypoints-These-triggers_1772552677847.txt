"No Chance" Neutrality: * Trigger: User selects "I didn't have a chance" in daily check-in.

The Bridge Lock: * Trigger: User is in "Standing Reset" mode.

Logic: The app should disable "Duration" negotiations until the user completes the 2-week Auto-Escalation back to walking.

When does it trigger?

It triggers only after a user has failed to do any walking for 2 consecutive weeks. At that point, the app "de-escalates" them to the Standing Reset (just 2 minutes of standing after dinner).



🛠️ How the Logic works:

Once the user is doing the Standing Reset, they are in the "Bridge." During this 2-week period:



No Negotiations: The Sunday Weekly Planner will not ask the user to increase their duration (e.g., "Can we go from 2 mins to 10 mins?") or add more days.

The Lock: All "Duration" and "Frequency" buttons are disabled or hidden in the UI.

The Goal: The only goal is to hit 100% success on those 2-minute "stands" to prove the habit is back on track.

🔓 How do they get out of the "Lock"?

The user must complete 2 weeks of 100% success on the Standing Reset.



The Auto-Escalation: Once they hit that 2-week mark, the app "unlocks" and automatically moves them back to a 5-minute walk.

Normal Service Resumes: Only after they are back to walking will the app start negotiating for +1 day or +5 minutes again.



⚙️ 4. Backend Sorting & Queue Keypoints

The Struggle Order: Ensure the app strictly follows the priority: Eat Out → Sugary Food/Drink → Snacks → Portion Control. If a user picks all, they must be queued in this exact sequence.

The "Ladder" Index: Each struggle has an array of tips. Debugging must ensure that a "Success" week increments the index by 1, while a "No" week keeps the index the same.

The "One Rest Day" Cap: The fatigue logic must have a hard constraint—it cannot suggest more than one permanent Rest Day per week, even if multiple days meet the 3/3 "Tired" criteria.
