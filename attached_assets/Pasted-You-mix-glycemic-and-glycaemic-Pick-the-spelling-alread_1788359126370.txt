2. Refine the quantity rule
This:

text
Do not claim carbohydrate quantity or glycaemic load without portion information.
could inadvertently prohibit qualitative statements such as “udon is a carbohydrate source.” Use:

text
Do not state an estimated carbohydrate amount, total carbohydrate burden, or glycaemic-load value without sufficient portion and composition information.
Then retain your later fallback:

text
When portion or composition data is unavailable, the advice may still describe a component qualitatively as a carbohydrate source, without stating an amount or glycaemic load.
That preserves the distinction you want: qualitative source identification is allowed; an amount, aggregate burden, or GL is not.

3. Make “supported” evidence-bound
This:

text
Consider the complete meal, portion, preparation, sauces/condiments, extras, food order, and supported mixed-meal effects.
is correct but vague. Use:

text
Consider the complete meal, available portion information, preparation, sauces/condiments, extras, food order, and evidence-supported mixed-meal effects. Do not present these effects as a precise glucose prediction.
The reason is important: mixed-meal response can be affected by protein, fat, fibre, acidity, food structure, and carbohydrate quantity, and a simplistic GI-based prediction is not reliable.
