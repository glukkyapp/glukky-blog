Risk Category 1: Species/Variety Conflation for Starchy Vegetables
Chinese yam isn't the only starchy vegetable where "same-sounding food name, different GI" can happen:

Food	GI range found	Why it varies
Taro (芋頭)	48–54, low	Consistent across sources 
Sweet potato (蕃薯)	44–94, huge spread	Depends heavily on cooking method (boiled vs. baked vs. instant) and variety 
Pumpkin (南瓜)	51–75, wide spread	Varies by species/region — Jamaican vs. South African boiled pumpkin differ by 24 points 
Yam species	44–104, extreme spread	Chinese yam (Dioscorea opposita) tests low; African pounded-yam varieties test very high 
The general principle: any starchy vegetable with multiple common species, cultivars, or cooking methods sharing one name is at risk of the same collapse into a single generic bucket. Sweet potato is the highest-risk case in Glukky's context, since its GI can range from "low" to "high" depending purely on how it's cooked — the same mashing/particle-size effect that applies to yam.

Risk Category 2: Low-Carb Proteins Wrongly Blamed for Meal Starch
Egg is one instance of a broader class: any near-zero-carbohydrate protein food can be misattributed a meal's starch/GL burden if the advice engine reasons at the wrong level.

Tofu: 1.9–3g carbs per 100g, "glycemic index framework doesn't apply directly" — glucose response comes from what it's served with, not the tofu itself.

Meat, fish, poultry: essentially zero carbohydrate, classified as "zero glycemic foods".

Cheese, plain yogurt (unsweetened): low-to-zero carbohydrate, same risk.

The general principle: any food dense in protein/fat with negligible carbohydrate is structurally incapable of contributing meaningfully to a meal's starch/glycemic load, so it should never be the subject of an aggregate-carb warning — that warning belongs exclusively to the meal's actual carbohydrate sources (rice, noodles, root vegetables, bread, sugary sauces).

A Single General Principle for the Ticket
Both risk categories collapse into one rule worth stating explicitly in ticket #865's evidence model: a food's glycemic classification must be tied to (a) its verified species/variety-specific evidence, and (b) its actual measured or estimated carbohydrate content — never inferred from category membership (root vegetable, tuber, "usually starchy") or from co-occurrence in a meal with genuinely starchy foods.

Concretely, this means the same fix protects against two failure directions at once — overclassifying a low/moderate-GI vegetable as high-impact due to species confusion (yam, pumpkin, sweet potato), and misattributing meal-level starch to a food that structurally can't carry it (egg, tofu, meat, cheese). Both are symptoms of the same missing piece: no structured, per-food, per-preparation, source-linked record — the exact gap ticket #865 is meant to close. Extending the regression suite to include at least one sweet-potato preparation-variant case and one tofu-in-a-carb-heavy-meal case would validate the fix generalizes beyond the yam/egg example.
