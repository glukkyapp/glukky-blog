# Source pool — launch articles

Every claim in the four launch articles maps to one of the sources below.
If a claim doesn't have a source here, it is either rephrased qualitatively
or removed.

## Foundational evidence — prediabetes / lifestyle change

1. **Knowler WC, et al. (Diabetes Prevention Program Research Group).**
   *Reduction in the Incidence of Type 2 Diabetes with Lifestyle Intervention or Metformin.*
   New England Journal of Medicine. 2002;346:393-403. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa012512.
   <https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa012512>
   - Used for: lifestyle change (modest weight loss + activity) reduced incidence
     of type 2 diabetes by 58% over ~3 years in adults with prediabetes,
     compared with 31% for metformin.

2. **NIDDK / NIH — Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP) overview.**
   <https://www.niddk.nih.gov/about-niddk/research-areas/diabetes/diabetes-prevention-program-dpp>
   - Used for: long-term follow-up showing benefit persists; intervention
     details (modest weight loss, ~150 min/week of moderate activity).

## Eating patterns

3. **Salas-Salvadó J, et al. (PREDIMED Study Investigators).**
   *Prevention of Diabetes With Mediterranean Diets: A Subgroup Analysis of a Randomized Trial.*
   Annals of Internal Medicine. 2014;160(1):1-10. DOI: 10.7326/M13-1725.
   <https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/M13-1725>
   - Used for: Mediterranean-style eating pattern associated with lower
     incidence of type 2 diabetes vs. low-fat control in adults at high
     cardiovascular risk.

4. **American Diabetes Association.**
   *Standards of Care in Diabetes — 2024.*
   Diabetes Care. 2024;47(Suppl. 1).
   <https://diabetesjournals.org/care/issue/47/Supplement_1>
   - Used for: general principles on whole grains, plant proteins,
     non-starchy vegetables; the "diabetes plate"; and individualised
     nutrition advice.

5. **World Health Organization — Healthy diet fact sheet.**
   <https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet>
   - Used for: limiting free sugars, replacing saturated/trans fats with
     unsaturated, eating fruit and vegetables daily.

6. **NHS — Type 2 diabetes (Prevention).**
   <https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/type-2-diabetes/>
   - Used for: practical guidance on weight, food choices, and activity
     for people at risk of type 2 diabetes.

7. **Hong Kong Centre for Health Protection — Diabetes Mellitus.**
   <https://www.chp.gov.hk/en/healthtopics/content/25/57.html>
   - Used for: Hong Kong context — diabetes is a leading non-communicable
     disease locally; lifestyle factors are central to prevention.

## Post-meal walking

8. **DiPietro L, Gribok A, Stevens MS, Hamm LF, Rumpler W.**
   *Three 15-min Bouts of Moderate Postmeal Walking Significantly Improves 24-h Glycemic Control in Older People at Risk for Impaired Glucose Tolerance.*
   Diabetes Care. 2013;36(10):3262-3268. DOI: 10.2337/dc13-0084.
   <https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/36/10/3262/30898>
   - Used for: a short walk shortly after meals improves 24-hour glucose
     control compared with one longer walk earlier in the day, in older
     adults at risk of impaired glucose tolerance.

9. **Reynolds AN, Mann JI, Williams S, Venn BJ.**
   *Advice to walk after meals is more effective for lowering postprandial glycaemia in type 2 diabetes mellitus than advice that does not specify timing: a randomised crossover study.*
   Diabetologia. 2016;59:2572-2578. DOI: 10.1007/s00125-016-4085-2.
   <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00125-016-4085-2>
   - Used for: in adults with type 2 diabetes, walking specifically after
     meals lowered postprandial glucose more than walking distributed
     across the day.

10. **Buffey AJ, Herring MP, Langley CK, Donnelly AE, Carson BP.**
    *The Acute Effects of Interrupting Prolonged Sitting With Brief Bouts of Light-Intensity Walking on Cardiometabolic Health: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis.*
    Sports Medicine. 2022;52:1765-1787. DOI: 10.1007/s40279-022-01649-4.
    <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-022-01649-4>
    - Used for: short bouts of light walking that interrupt sitting can
      lower glucose and insulin responses compared with prolonged sitting.

11. **Hong Kong Department of Health — Exercise Prescription.**
    <https://www.chp.gov.hk/en/static/100013.html>
    - Used for: HK adult activity recommendations (≥150 minutes of
      moderate-intensity activity per week).
